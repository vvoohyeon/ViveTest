Every specimen before this one showed a rounded floating surface, which is the desktop shape and the opposite of this rule.

Measured on the running product at 390 × 844, 2026-09-07. The specimen above draws its sheet flush for lack of a grid to sit in; the 281.59px row is the honest version of where it actually appears.

Wave 13 — "Mobile expanded shape/position" — was never implemented, and BQ-38 superseded it. So the three gaps above are not defects to fix; they are the difference between a finished shape nobody built and a realized transient-shell implementation that works. Decision C freezes the catalog's patterns, so this card records the gap and does not design it closed. The design pass gets both columns.
The 40px close button is the one that is not merely unfinished — design.md §4.10 names the close button among the controls that must be at least 44 × 44, so it is an accessibility gap against the foundations rather than an unbuilt wave. Raising it is a runtime change and belongs to the surface work.

Reading the source alone gets the radius wrong. landing-grid-card-mobile-transient-shell carries rounded-[var(--landing-card-radius)], which suggests a 16px radius — but that class is the OPENING / CLOSING shell, anchored at the card's own left and width. The steady OPEN state is a different element and is full-bleed at radius 0. The running product is what settled it.

Behaviour, from req-landing.md §8.5. Lifecycle is one-way: OPENING → OPEN → CLOSING → NORMAL, and a tap that started OPENING may not reverse into CLOSING in the same sequence. The X is a single icon at the header's right end, visible from the start of OPENING until just before CLOSING ends and inert during CLOSING. Closing is the X or a backdrop tap, and nothing else — §7.8 declines to authorize swipe-down. The active card's top y-anchor must not drift during either transition. Layer order is GNB > expanded > backdrop > other cards, and the backdrop never covers the expanded card.

Never: a side radius, a floating margin, a shadow reading as elevation, a second close path, or type scaled by the desktop shell factor — mobile has no shell scale, and the question is 21px on screen rather than 21 × 1.04.
