D-03 — resolved

The finding was not that the values were wrong; it was that they had no consumer. --accent-hover and --accent-pressed were declared and derived, and nothing in the system or the product ever read --accent-pressed. It now belongs to the pressed state of .vt-cta — the one control that carries the filled accent — where the lift returns to zero in the same step, so the button goes down and darker at once.

One rule covers both themes. Interaction moves the accent one step toward higher contrast with the ground. On a light ground that is darker — 500 → 600 → 700. On a dark ground it is lighter — 400 → 300 → 200. Written as "one shade darker", the rule silently inverts to less visible in dark, which is why the README now states it as a direction rather than as a value. The dark theme also anchors one step off the light one: --sage-500 measures 3.75:1 on white and 4.59:1 on the dark ground, so it cannot serve both; --sage-400 gives dark 6.63:1 on the card.

D-12 — the accent has two jobs

Found while checking the dark side, and it was the light side that was broken. As a line, ring or tint the accent sits beside text and the bar is 3:1, which --accent clears at 3.75. As the fill under a label the bar is 4.5:1 against that label — and white on --accent is 3.75:1, so the submit CTA's own text had been failing AA at rest.

Inverting the label does not fix it. A dark label measures 4.61 at rest and then falls to 3.39 and 2.44 as the button deepens on hover and press — the fill moves toward the label, so interaction makes it worse. Stepping the fill one deeper instead keeps the white label and moves the right way: 5.09 → 7.09 → 9.76. Hence --accent-solid, --accent-solid-hover and --accent-solid-pressed, which a filled accent control reads instead of --accent.

The label itself is theme-aware. It was #fff, hardcoded — 2.42:1 on the dark theme's lighter accent, an outright failure. It now reads --fg-on-accent: white in light, --warm-950 in dark.
