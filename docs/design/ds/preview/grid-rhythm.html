Three of the properties that make a row of catalog cards look deliberate live in the grid, not in any card — the gutter, the column count, and equal height — and a fourth, the common line every tags row sits on, is produced by a number written onto each card individually. None of them could be reproduced from this system before, because the system described a card and never the thing cards are arranged in.

With the mechanisms

Four Desktop Wide later-row cards at 292px, 24px gutter, deliberately unequal content: two one-line subtitles against two two-line, and tag counts of 2, 4, 1, 3. All four cards are the same height and all four tags rows sit on one line.

The compensation is authored per card, and the numbers above are illustrative rather than measured. The product computes them: spacing-plan.ts:1 fixes the base at 8px, :142 zeroes the compensation whenever needs_comp is false, and landing-grid-card.tsx:1144-1145 writes both onto the card as inline custom properties which :220 then adds together. req-landing.md:327-334 requires a needs_comp = false card to hold exactly 0px and never to pass through a positive value for even one frame — which is why the second and fourth cards read 0px rather than a small number.

The column count is not one number per band. Wide (>=1160) puts 3 in the first row and 4 in every row after it; Medium (1040–1159) puts 2 then 3; below 1040 every non-mobile row is 2 with no first-row exception; at or below 767px it is a single mobile column. Both card widths in this bundle are Desktop Wide on a 1240px grid — (1240 − 2×24) / 3 = 397 for a first-row card and (1240 − 3×24) / 4 = 292 for a later-row one. req-landing.md §6.2.

Never: a fixed minimum, a floor, or a spacer element standing in for the compensation — req-landing.md §6 bans all three by name. No dynamic margin or pseudo-element below the tags. No axis alignment that breaks the row's stretch. And the tags slot is always the last one in the collapsed face.
